Clinical NLP and health informatics

How health information represents people, and how AI uses it.

I study clinical language, documentation equity, and the evaluation of AI for health. My work asks how records describe patients, what context is lost when those records are summarized, and how we can check the evidence behind a model’s answer.

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Tennessee Health Science Center. I received my PhD in Library and Information Science from Rutgers University in January 2026, where I worked with Vivek Singh in the Behavioral Informatics Lab.

I am on the 2026–2027 faculty job market. Please get in touch about research and teaching opportunities.

Current research

X-CART 2.0 Memory Engine

Clinical memory and evidence retrieval

I lead a study of how to reconstruct a patient’s changing history from clinical notes while preserving the source of each assertion. We evaluate the prototype on synthetic oncology histories and compare evidence retrieval on real discharge summaries. The work examines what a model can support from the records available at a given time.

Manuscript in preparation for submission.

Repeat assessments for mental health monitoring

Questionnaire allocation and measurement

In this collaboration, we study whom to ask for a new questionnaire when collection capacity is limited. Retrospective simulations compare uncertainty-based selection with random allocation and examine how collection opportunities and prediction errors vary across demographic groups.

Manuscript in preparation for submission.

Breast cancer care and disparities in Memphis

Neighborhood conditions and access to care

This collaborative study examines racial and neighborhood differences in breast cancer outcomes. I will lead planned analyses of where patients receive care and how service use varies across neighborhoods, using administrative claims and facility data.

RAG Repair Transfer

Evidence selection for question answering

I collaborate on methods for selecting and revising evidence for multi-hop question answering. The study compares model performance across benchmark datasets under limited evidence budgets. A manuscript is in preparation.
